Planning checklist

Keep night shifts clear before they begin

Overnight work crosses calendar dates, which makes a simple schedule surprisingly easy to misread. A consistent entry method keeps start times, reminders, locations and pay details attached to the right shift.

Published and reviewed August 1, 2026

Created and reviewed by My Shift Planner

Based on direct product implementation, testing and current app behavior.

Use the start date as the shift date

For personal calendar planning, a night shift that starts at 19:00 on Monday and finishes at 07:00 on Tuesday is usually easiest to record on Monday. Store both times so the duration crosses midnight correctly. Follow your employer's convention if its official schedule uses a different rule.

A seven-point night shift checklist

  1. Confirm the start date and time. Check whether the roster lists the start day or finishing day.
  2. Record the end time. Make sure the planner treats an earlier clock time as the following day.
  3. Add the correct workplace. This matters when locations, units or roles change.
  4. Check the reminder. Set it before the start time, not on the following calendar morning.
  5. Review consecutive nights. A monthly view makes work blocks and gaps easier to see.
  6. Record eligible differentials. Use only the hours and rules supplied by your employer.
  7. Confirm exceptions. Meetings, training, on-call periods and overtime may need separate records.
Cross-midnight example

Monday 19:00 to Tuesday 07:00 is one 12-hour shift beginning Monday. It should not appear as two separate shifts unless your workplace records it that way.

Separate work reminders from personal recovery time

Your work planner can tell you when and where a shift begins. Personal calendar blocks can separately protect commuting, meals and rest. Keeping those purposes distinct prevents an official-looking work schedule from becoming cluttered.

Do not rely on color alone

Use a night-shift label or symbol as well as a color. Labels remain understandable in exported reports, widgets and accessibility settings, and they reduce ambiguity when several shift types use similar tones.

Check hours and pay as a second step

After the calendar is correct, review total hours and any night or weekend differential. Pay estimates are useful for planning, but final eligibility, overtime and payroll calculations come from your employer.
