Rotation guide

Common shift rotation patterns

There is no single “best” shift pattern. The useful question is whether a rotation is predictable, matches your actual workplace schedule and leaves enough context in your calendar to understand what happens next.

Published and reviewed August 1, 2026

Created and reviewed by My Shift Planner

Based on direct product implementation, testing and current app behavior.

Start with the schedule you actually receive

A personal shift planner should reflect your employer's official roster, not replace it. Before building a repeating pattern, confirm the start date, shift length, day or night assignment and whether the sequence changes after holidays, leave or training.

Useful rule

Anchor every rotation to one confirmed workday. If the anchor date is wrong, every later date in the repeating pattern will also be wrong.

Five patterns you may encounter

Fixed schedule

Same working days

You work a consistent set of weekdays or nights. It is easy to review, but exceptions still need to be recorded separately.

2-2-3 pattern

Two on, two off, three on

A two-week cycle often alternates shorter and longer work blocks. Exact day/night arrangements vary by employer.

4-on/4-off

Four workdays, four rest days

The cycle moves through calendar weekdays over time. It can be predictable while rarely matching a Monday-to-Friday month.

Rotating days and nights

Shift time changes

The work block may stay consistent while start times rotate. Label day and night assignments explicitly instead of relying only on color.

Custom cycle

Employer-specific sequence

Many workplaces use local variations. Record the exact sequence rather than assuming that a familiar pattern name has one universal definition.

How to choose a planner setup

  1. Write the sequence in plain language. Example: two day shifts, two days off, three night shifts.
  2. Confirm shift boundaries. A night shift beginning Monday may finish Tuesday, but it usually belongs to the Monday start date in a work calendar.
  3. Add exceptions after the pattern. Training, meetings, overtime, leave and cancelled work should remain visible rather than silently changing the rotation.
  4. Review two full cycles. Compare the generated calendar with the official roster before relying on future dates.
  5. Use reminders selectively. Reminders are most useful for actual work or on-call records, not leave or cancelled entries.

What to compare between rotations

Look beyond the number of working days. Compare consecutive shifts, the direction of day/night changes, weekend frequency, cross-midnight work, expected hours and how often the pattern is interrupted. If you work at more than one location, also check for overlaps and travel time.

Use rotation presets carefully

A preset saves repetitive entry, but it should remain editable. Real schedules change because of swaps, leave, overtime or staffing needs. Treat the preset as a starting structure and keep the calendar aligned with the official schedule.
